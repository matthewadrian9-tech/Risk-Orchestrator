MANDATE

US long/short, consumer and industrials. Gross to 200%, net -20% to +40%.

THESIS

Input cost deflation does not reach shareholders evenly, and the market keeps
assuming it does. When grain, freight and packaging costs fall, the businesses
that sell a commodity into a tight processing market keep the spread, because
capacity is what constrains them and capacity has not been added. The businesses
that sell a branded box into a supermarket give it straight back, because their
retail partner has a private label alternative on the next shelf and the price
gap is the only thing keeping units moving. The last two years proved this in
the numbers and the multiples have not adjusted. What has to be true is that
input costs stay down for another two quarters and that private label share
keeps climbing rather than plateauing.

EXPRESSION

Long the spread capturers. TSN on protein processing where the margin is
capacity-constrained rather than price-constrained, TJX on off-price where
inventory availability improves as branded volumes weaken, ZTS on animal health
where pricing has held through the cycle. NDAQ, GEN and ATO are unrelated
positions I hold for their own reasons and they are not part of this argument.

Short the givers-back: CL and KMB where private label is taking share outright,
CAG on volume declines running six quarters, WMT as the party extracting the
saving rather than keeping it, ED and VZ as funding shorts.

CATALYSTS

Fourth-quarter volume disclosure across the short book in February. Grain and
freight cost prints, monthly. TJX comparable store sales in late February.

RISKS

A single quarter of volume stabilisation in packaged food squeezes the short
book hard, and it is a crowded short. If input costs spike again the entire
argument reverses inside a quarter.
