MANDATE

Global industrials and technology infrastructure long/short. Gross to 200%, net between -20% and +40%, no single name above 8% of NAV. No credit, no listed options without desk approval.

THESIS

The constraint on datacenter growth is not chips, it is electricity and the equipment that moves it. Interconnection queues in the major US markets now run four to six years, transformer lead times have gone from forty weeks to over two years, and none of that resolves on a normal capex cycle because the supply base was consolidated during a decade of flat utility spending. The market is still valuing the equipment layer as late-cycle industrials that mean-revert. They are not: these are multi-year backlogs at prices the vendors set. What has to be true is that hyperscaler capex guidance holds through the next two reporting seasons and that utilities keep placing orders rather than deferring.

EXPRESSION

Long the equipment and power layer - VRT, ETN, PWR, CEG, and SMCI as the one box-level name where the backlog is contracted rather than indicated. I am deliberately not long the obvious semiconductor end names; that consensus is crowded and prices the cycle already.

The short side funds the book with consumer staples whose multiples were bid up as bond proxies and whose volume growth is negative.

CATALYSTS

Q3 hyperscaler earnings, late October, should revise capex guidance upward again. FERC interconnection queue data in December. Two transformer suppliers report mid-January and either confirm or break the backlog story.

RISKS

Book-to-bill below 1.0 at VRT or ETN impairs the thesis, not just dents it. A single hyperscaler cutting capex guidance in Q1 would take the whole equipment complex down together, which is the concentration risk I am accepting.
