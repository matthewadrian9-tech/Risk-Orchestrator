MANDATE

Japan long/short, sector agnostic. Gross to 180%, net -20% to +40%. No single name above 8% of NAV.

THESIS

The Tokyo Stock Exchange's pressure on companies trading below book value has moved from advisory to something with consequences, and the trading houses and industrials are responding with buybacks and cross-shareholding unwinds at a pace that is not yet in consensus numbers. The mechanism is simple and boring: a company sells the shares it holds in its customers, uses the proceeds to retire its own stock, and return on equity rises without anything changing operationally. This is not a growth story and does not need one.

EXPRESSION

Long the trading houses and heavy industrials with the largest cross-holding books relative to market capitalisation - 8058, 8001, 7011, 6301. Short domestic telecom and beverage, where governance is already good, the balance sheet is already efficient, and there is no similar catalyst to come.

CATALYSTS

Mid-year results in early November, where buyback authorisations are typically announced. TSE follow-up disclosure on the below-book companies expected in the first quarter.

RISKS

A stronger yen compresses trading house earnings faster than buybacks can offset. If the TSE softens the disclosure requirement the pressure disappears and this becomes a value trap.
